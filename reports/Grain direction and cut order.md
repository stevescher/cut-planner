# Make grain and cut order honest tradeoffs

Woodworkers treat grain as a **per-part visibility decision layered on a per-material default**, and they treat cut order as a **labor and handling choice that should cost as little material as possible**. Comparable apps already converge on that shape for grain: a sheet-level grain flag (usually "grain follows length") plus a per-part override in both directions, meaning lock a part to grain or free it from grain. For cut order, they converge on a first-cut direction setting whose default is "let the optimizer decide". The loudest user complaint is not parts rotated against grain. It is the reverse: **grain forced onto MDF, painted work and hidden parts, wasting whole sheets**, with one Easel user reporting that his hand layout beat the grain-forced plan by 4 sheets. On cut order, users complain about layouts that are impractical at the saw (identical parts scattered, too many fence moves) more than about the missing setting itself. **No app found shows the user what a grain or cut-order choice costs in sheets or dollars**, even though users already make that comparison by hand. For cut-planner, that gap is the clearest opening: ship grain as a three-state sheet property with tri-state part overrides, ship cut order as an Auto / Favor / Always scale, and show the sheet and price delta of every constraint next to the result.

## Grain follows the long side, and only visible parts care

On standard veneer-faced plywood, face grain **runs along the long (8 ft / 2440 mm) dimension**; one supplier's rule of thumb is "the grain follows the bigger number" regardless of the order the dimensions are written ([Plyco](https://help.plyco.com.au/en/knowledge/which-direction-does-the-grain-run)), and structural-panel face plies run parallel to the 8 ft side ([Wikipedia: Plywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plywood)). Cross-grain sheets are real but sold as **distinct SKUs**, for example flexible plywood listed as "Cross Grain 1220mm x 2440mm (8' x 4')" next to a long-grain version ([Sheet Materials Wholesale](https://sheetmaterialswholesale.co.uk/flexible-plywood-cross-grain-5-x-1220-x-2440mm); [Strahan](https://strahanonlineshop.ie/products/flexi-cross-grain-plywood)). One low-authority page claims that writing "8x4" instead of "4x8" signals cross grain ([arhfoundation.org](https://www.arhfoundation.org/plywood-dimensions-width-by-length)), which contradicts Plyco's rule; an app should not infer grain from the order a user types dimensions. Baltic birch is commonly sold **5' x 5' (1525 x 1525 mm)** ([Capitol City Lumber](https://www.capitolcitylumber.com/product/5x5-baltic-birch-plywood/); [MakerStock](https://makerstock.com/products/baltic-birch-plywood-full-sheet-local-pickup)), and no source found states which edge its face grain parallels, so on square sheets the "long side" default has no answer and the user has to choose. Woodgrain melamine/TFL counts as grained for layout purposes ([Thermwood forum](https://www.thermwood.com/forums/viewtopic.php?p=62768)). The notes contain no source that explicitly lists raw MDF, particleboard or solid-color melamine as grainless; that classification is an inference, though it is reinforced by an Easel user's point that MDF has no grain ([Easel forum](https://forum.easel.com/t/cut-list-optimization/151499)).

At the part level, the dividing line is visibility. A cabinetmaker said he **"could have saved 3 sheets on a 19 sheet job"** by freeing grain on toe kicks and stretchers. He also rejected the workaround of assigning those parts a separate grainless material, because that creates a separate nest that cannot use leftover space on the grained sheets ([Thermwood forum](https://www.thermwood.com/forums/viewtopic.php?p=62768)). That point matters for design: per-part grain freedom has to live inside the same material, not as a material swap. A Microvellum user with 50+ parts per desk unit, about half of which needed manual grain changes, asked for grain to be auto-assigned by material and part size ([Microvellum Community](https://community.microvellum.com/portal/en/community/topic/feature-request-bring-back-the-ability-for-sma-smt-to-apply-width-length-grain-direction-based-on-overall-part-size)). Shelf strength along the grain is real in principle, but woodworkers treat it as secondary: a front lip and a shorter span matter far more (doubling span raises sag roughly 8x) ([Woodweb](https://woodweb.com/knowledge_base/Stiffening_LongSpan_Bookshelves.html); [FWW forum](https://www.finewoodworking.com/forum/grain-direction-of-shelf), snippet only). Grain continuity is its own category: drawer fronts are cut in sequence from one strip so the figure flows across the bank ([Fine Woodworking](https://finewoodworking.com/2021/05/18/create-a-continuous-grain-apron-and-drawer), snippet only), and pro tools ship explicit grouping features for it, covered below.

## Rip-first dominates table saws, but no order is universal

A rip runs with the grain and a crosscut runs across it ([Columbia Forest Products](https://www.columbiaforestproducts.com/blog/sawing-plywood)); on a sheet, that usually means a rip is along the long dimension. The canonical table-saw sequence is **rip to rough width off the factory edge, crosscut to rough length, rip to finished width, then crosscut to finished length**, with "the factory edge will always be trimmed away" ([Woodsmith](https://www.woodsmith.com/article/perfect-plywood-panels-on-the-table-saw-rough-to-final-size/)). Sliding table saw practice often runs the other way: crosscut the rough edge first, square a corner off the crosscut fence, then rip ([Woodweb: Sliding Table Saw Tips](https://woodweb.com/knowledge_base/Sliding_Table_Saw_Tips_and_Tricks.html)). Production shops split the work across machines, ripping on a vertical panel saw and sending strips to a crosscut beam saw ([Woodweb: Beam Saw Cut Quality](https://woodweb.com/knowledge_base/Beam_Saw_Cut_Quality.html)).

The hobbyist debate is genuinely two-sided. On a Festool Owners Group thread, rip-first advocates said **"a 21 x 96 piece [is] easier to handle than a 48 x 31 3/4"**, while crosscut-first advocates said it preserves larger usable offcuts (one 3x4 ft piece instead of four 1x3 ft scraps); the consensus was that it depends on how the parts lay out ([Festool Owners Group](https://festoolownersgroup.com/threads/help-rip-cut-or-crosscut-first-on-sheetgoods.6815)). Track saw users commonly make the first cut along the long edge to establish a reference ([TSO Products](https://tsoproducts.com/blog/types-of-track-saw-cuts-and-how-to-do-them-right/), snippet only). Pro shops put numbers on the tradeoff: in a 2009 WOODWEB discussion, rip-first was described as about **20% faster**, while optimizer material savings on standard jobs were only **2 to 4%**, and one shop said labor savings "were far more valuable" on $27 melamine but would optimize harder on expensive veneered ply ([WOODWEB](https://woodweb.com/knowledge_base/Optimizing_Materials_Versus_Speed.html)). That source is old and small, but it is the only full-text pro discussion in the notes, and it frames the core point: **the right cut order depends on the price of the sheet**, which is exactly the variable an app can show.

Professional software formalizes this as staged guillotine cutting. Microvellum defines a phase as "the number of times a piece of material can be cut and rotated and re-cut," supports 2 to 4 phases, and offers a five-step first-phase scale: **Always Rip, Favor Rip, Auto, Favor Cross, Always Cross**, where "Favor Rip" tries rip first "but may cross-cut if yielding better results" ([Microvellum](https://intercom.help/microvellum-knowledge-base/en/articles/14858797-microvellum-saw-optimization-settings-reference)). Tearout is mostly a tool and blade concern (good face down under a circular or track saw, high tooth count, zero clearance, scoring) rather than a cut-order concern ([Columbia Forest Products](https://www.columbiaforestproducts.com/blog/sawing-plywood)); its main tie to ordering is the rough-then-finish pattern, which trims away a chipped rough edge.

## Competing apps agree on structure but never show the cost

### Grain settings split into two models

The apps examined use one of two grain models. CutList Optimizer is the outlier: a global **"Consider grain direction"** checkbox reveals a three-state symbol on both sheets and panels (along length, across width, don't care), exported as V / H / blank in CSV ([Product Design Online](https://productdesignonline.com/tips-and-tricks/adventures-in-panel-cutting/)), and grain is off by default in its config (`considerOrientation: false`) ([CutList Optimizer](https://www.cutlistoptimizer.com/)). Most other tools treat grain as a boolean on the material that always follows sheet length, plus a per-part override.

| App | Sheet / material grain | Per-part control | Grain matching |
|---|---|---|---|
| CutList Optimizer | Global toggle; sheet grain along length or across width ([PDO](https://productdesignonline.com/tips-and-tricks/adventures-in-panel-cutting/)) | Along length / across width / don't care | None found |
| MaxCut | "Has Grain" checkbox; grain always follows length ([MaxCut](https://knowledge.maxcutsoftware.com/doc/setting-a-materials-grain-slash-graphic-direction)) | "No Rotation" under Advanced; per-panel ignore option reported (snippet) | "Match Grain on Selected Panels" (Ctrl+G), strip and grain orientation per group ([MaxCut](https://knowledge.maxcutsoftware.com/doc/how-to-match-grain-in-maxcut)) |
| OpenCutList | Sheet good has grain or not; grain runs along length ([docs](https://docs.opencutlist.org/features/applying-materials.md)) | "Ignore grain orientation on this part"; grained parts rotate only 180° ([docs](https://docs.opencutlist.org/features/parts/parts-list/cutting-diagrams.md)) | Algorithm supports groups, no UI (snippet) ([lairdubois](https://lairdubois.fr/questions/reponses/19641)) |
| OptiCut (Boole) | Grain follows longer side by default | "Upon material" / "No" / "Yes" / "Reverse grain" ([Wood Designer](https://wooddesigner.org/help-centre/opticut-create-cutting-list/)) | "Perfect grain" groups adjacent fronts ([Wood Designer](https://wooddesigner.org/help-centre/opticut-perfect-grain-management/)) |
| SmartCut | Stock never rotates; length is the grain axis | `orientationLock`: 'l', 'w', or free ([SmartCut](https://smartcut.dev/docs/guide)) | None found |
| Fusion Arrange | Grain along envelope length by default | "Global" / "All Rotations" / "None" / "180 Only" / "90/270 Only" ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-CAM/files/NST-ARR-ADV-ROTATE.htm)) | None found |
| Microvellum | Grain by material | Per-part grain | "Perfect Grain": group separately, or cut in the initial sheet ([Microvellum](https://intercom.help/microvellum-knowledge-base/en/articles/14858797-microvellum-saw-optimization-settings-reference)) |
| Easel Cabinetmaker | Always on, no off switch as of Feb 2024 ([Easel forum](https://forum.easel.com/t/cut-list-optimization/151499)) | None | None |

Two distinct per-part semantics appear across these tools, and a complete design needs both. **"Lock"** (MaxCut No Rotation, SmartCut 'l'/'w', CutPlan's lock toggle ([CutPlan](https://cutplan.ai/en/blog/how-to-use-cut-list-optimizer-tutorial.html))) pins a part even on grainless stock. **"Exempt"** (OpenCutList's ignore, OptiCut's "No") frees a part on grained stock. SmartCut and OptiCut also let a part's grain run along its width ("w", "Reverse grain"), which covers a part whose figure runs across its short dimension. MaxCut's single setting covers both "grain / graphic direction", treating printed and patterned sheets the same as wood ([MaxCut](https://knowledge.maxcutsoftware.com/doc/setting-a-materials-grain-slash-graphic-direction)). Grain matching is a pro and mid-tier feature that arrived late: OptiCut users were told in July 2019 that matching across panels was "not yet possible" ([Wood Designer forum](https://wooddesigner.org/forum/opticut-optinest/grain-matching/)), and the common industry pattern now is a matched group placed as one strip or block with its own strip and grain orientation (MaxCut's "Strip Follows Panel Length / Width").

### First-cut direction is everywhere, under three vocabularies

| App | Label | Values | Hard or soft |
|---|---|---|---|
| CutList Optimizer | "Cut Orientation" | "Optimal", "Prefer cuts along the width", "Prefer cuts along the length" ([labels-en.js](https://www.cutlistoptimizer.com/js/labels/labels-en.js?v=1.12.1)) | Wording says soft; behavior undocumented |
| OpenCutList / PackingSolver | "First Cut Direction" | "Horizontal", "Vertical", "Any" (default Any); 2 or 3 stages (default 3) ([en.yml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lairdubois/lairdubois-opencutlist-sketchup-extension/master/src/ladb_opencutlist/yaml/i18n-src/en.yml); [defaults](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lairdubois/lairdubois-opencutlist-sketchup-extension/master/src/ladb_opencutlist/json/defaults/cutlist_packing_options.json)) | Hard: part of the problem definition ([PackingSolver](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fontanf/packingsolver/master/README.md)) |
| MaxCut | "Optimisation Method" | "Normal", "Multistage (Following Length)", "Multistage (Following Width)" ([MaxCut](https://knowledge.maxcutsoftware.com/doc/optimisation-method-settings)) | Hard, applied job-wide |
| OptiCut | "First cut direction" | "Indifferent", vertical, horizontal ([Wood Designer](https://wooddesigner.org/help-centre/opticut-optimization-parameters/)) | Hard when fixed |
| Microvellum | "Phase 1" | Always Rip / Favor Rip / Auto / Favor Cross / Always Cross ([Microvellum](https://intercom.help/microvellum-knowledge-base/en/articles/14858797-microvellum-saw-optimization-settings-reference)) | Both, explicitly |
| Mozaik | First cuts | Rip Cuts / Cross Cuts / Three Phase Cutting (search summary only) ([Mozaik](https://www.mozaiksoftware.com/products/mozaik-manufacturing/plus-optimizer)) | Unknown |
| Rogue Engineer | None | Always rip-first, same-width parts grouped into strips ([Rogue Engineer](https://rogueengineer.com/cut-list-optimizer/)) | Hard, not configurable |

The vocabulary falls into three families: geometric (horizontal / vertical), sheet-relative (length / width), and woodworking (rip / crosscut). Rip / crosscut matches the user's mental model at the saw, but it only means something when the sheet has a grain axis; on MDF, length / width is unambiguous. Wherever a default could be confirmed, it is "let the optimizer decide." **Microvellum is the only tool found that offers both hard ("Always") and soft ("Favor") semantics**. Because a constrained search can never beat an unconstrained one, every hard direction constraint costs material or ties, yet **none of the documented tools reports that delta**, and no grain doc examined (MaxCut, OpenCutList, OptiCut, SmartCut, Fusion, CutList Optimizer) offers a with-versus-without comparison. The closest thing is advice text: CutPlan warns that "over-locking is a common self-inflicted wound" ([CutPlan](https://cutplan.ai/en/blog/how-to-use-cut-list-optimizer-tutorial.html)). That absence is inferred from documentation, not from hands-on testing of every app.

Two adjacent capabilities show up in user requests but rarely in hobby tools. Stage limits are common (OpenCutList's 2 or 3, Microvellum's 2 to 4 phases), but a **fence or first-cut capacity limit** appears only in PackingSolver's engine parameters (`max1cut`), which OpenCutList does not expose ([PackingSolver](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fontanf/packingsolver/master/README.md)). A MaxCut user whose guillotine "has a maximum of 91 cm" asked for exactly that, with no visible reply ([MaxCut feedback](https://feedback.maxcutsoftware.com/p/setting-the-maximum-size-of-the-first-cut-VtXf0I)). Another MaxCut user, on 60+ sheet jobs, asked that the first cut not have to be the same on every sheet ([MaxCut feedback](https://feedback.maxcutsoftware.com/p/optimisation-method-specifying-that-for-the-whole-job-first--j4TeQI)), which amounts to a request for per-sheet "Favor" behavior rather than a job-wide hard rule.

## Users want fewer forced constraints and layouts they can actually cut

The user evidence points one way on grain: **constraints applied too broadly**. The Easel user who saved 4 sheets by cutting manually described "huge parts of the sheet not being used" and asked to turn grain off ([Easel forum: Grain Direction](https://forum.easel.com/t/grain-direction/152558)). Another Easel user spelled out the rule woodworkers actually follow, grain for drawer fronts and sides but not stretchers and nailers and not MDF, and said CutList Optimizer handled selective grain better ([Easel forum](https://forum.easel.com/t/cut-list-optimization/151499)). A Festool user noted that painted cabinets free up orientation, giving choices that "save material, but complicate the cuts" ([Festool Owners Group](https://festoolownersgroup.com/threads/sheet-goods-layout-program.67481)). OpenCutList's per-part rotation override on grained stock was added in v2.0.1 with UI contributed by a community member, a sign that this was user-driven (snippet only) ([OpenCollective](https://opencollective.com/lairdubois-opencutlist-sketchup-extension/updates/opencutlist-version-2-0-1-released)). The secondary complaint is setup confusion: an OpenCutList user called the process of getting grain right "overcomplicated" ([SketchUp forum](https://forums.sketchup.com/t/is-grain-direction-supposed-to-work-that-way/253179)). The research found **no user post complaining that a tool rotated a visible part against the grain**; whether that is rare or simply not surfaced by search is unknown.

On cut order, users judge layouts at the saw. The same Easel user objected that six identical 25.5 in. parts were spread across three columns with "zero" lined up for a crosscut ([Easel forum](https://forum.easel.com/t/cut-list-optimization/151499)). A Fine Woodworking reviewer reportedly wanted a "minimum number of fence settings" mode for CutList Optimizer, and a Sawmill Creek poster reportedly wanted adjacent placement for grain continuity; both pages returned HTTP 403, so these come only from search snippets and carry lower confidence ([FWW](https://finewoodworking.com/2021/07/20/my-experience-with-cutlistoptimizer-com); [Sawmill Creek](https://sawmillcreek.org/goto/post?id=2899815)). A one-person shop cutting 56 sheets for 45 cabinets asked to optimize a few sheets at a time with each cabinet's parts together to "reduce the number of saw set ups" ([MaxCut feedback](https://feedback.maxcutsoftware.com/p/optimize-by-input-item-groups-HEpjCe)). Newer tools market guillotine-only, rip-first strip grouping as the fix for layouts "no table saw can actually cut" ([Rogue Engineer](https://rogueengineer.com/cut-list-optimizer/)). Grain matching demand is old and real, including a 2012 objection that an optimizer would break the "uniform grain pattern" a manual layout preserved ([Sawdust Zone](https://www.sawdustzone.org/forum/discussions/project-related-discussions/56326-plywood-optimization?p=712980)), but no posts surfaced asking for door-pair, waterfall or book-match support.

No user post was found asking a tool to show the sheet or dollar cost of a grain or cut-order choice. The case for cost visibility is inferred from behavior (users comparing manual layouts by hand, pros reasoning about labor versus material by sheet price), not from a stated request. Evidence gaps are significant: **Reddit content could not be retrieved at all**, Fine Woodworking and Sawmill Creek were blocked, and the pro sample is small and partly from 2009.

## Recommendations for cut-planner

Everything in this section is design judgment built on the findings above, not sourced fact. Where it refers to the current app, the claims were checked against the source code on 2026-10-06.

### What the code does today

The sheet form offers only "length" and "width" buttons and treats an unset value as length (`(sheet.grainDirection ?? 'length')` in `src/components/forms/StockSheetForm.tsx`), so **there is no way to say a sheet has no grain**. The only references to `grainDirection` under `src/lib/optimizer/` are in `types.ts`, so the solver never reads sheet grain. Per the convention documented in `src/lib/grain.ts`, an unrotated piece is placed with its length on the X axis (sheet length). A `lockRotation` panel therefore keeps its length along the sheet's length even when that sheet is set to grain-along-width, which is the opposite of what the lock's tooltip ("Click to lock grain direction", `PanelForm.tsx`) implies. The split rules `'horizontal-first'` and `'vertical-first'` in `guillotine.ts` return the same split direction at every placement, not only the first cut, so they behave like a fixed split policy rather than a first-stage orientation. Each sheet already carries an optional `pricePerSheet`, which makes a dollar cost delta possible without new inputs.

### Feature 1: grain as a real, three-state constraint

Make sheet grain a three-state property, **Along length / Along width / None**, and keep "None" as the default for new sheets. That matches CutList Optimizer's off-by-default config and the user evidence that forced grain is the main complaint. Offer material presets that pre-fill it (veneered ply and woodgrain TFL to Along length, MDF / particleboard / solid melamine / hardboard to None), and when the sheet is square, require an explicit choice rather than defaulting. Do not infer grain from the order of the typed dimensions.

Replace the `lockRotation` boolean with a per-panel **Grain** setting that has three values, and translate it to the solver as an allowed-orientation set, the way SmartCut and Fusion do:

| Panel setting | On a grained sheet | On a grainless sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Follow grain (default when sheet has grain) | Part length parallel to sheet grain | Rotation allowed |
| Grain across part | Part width parallel to sheet grain (OptiCut "Reverse grain", SmartCut 'w') | Rotation allowed |
| Any orientation | Rotation allowed (hidden parts) | Rotation allowed |

Keep a separate "lock as drawn" only if a real need for it appears on grainless stock (CutPlan, MaxCut and SmartCut support it, mainly for printed sheets and pattern alignment); otherwise the three values cover the documented cases. Migration is direct: existing `lockRotation: true` panels map to "Follow grain", and existing sheets with `grainDirection` set keep it, while sheets with it unset should be treated as "None" only after deciding whether old projects relied on the implicit "length" default. Because the solver currently ignores sheet grain, fixing the width-grain case (rotate the allowed set when the sheet grain is along width) is a correctness fix that belongs in the same change. Per the Thermwood evidence, freeing a part must keep it in the same nest as its grained siblings; do not implement "Any orientation" as a material swap. Render crossed grain using the existing `isGrainCrossed` overlay, and flag only parts whose setting forbids the orientation shown.

**Grain-matched groups** are worth scoping as a second phase. The industry pattern is clear (MaxCut, OptiCut, Microvellum, CADMate all place a matched group as one strip or block cut in sequence), and it fits the guillotine solver naturally: treat the group as one composite rectangle sized to the parts plus kerf, place it like any panel, then split it. Expose it as "Match grain" on a selection of panels, with a strip orientation (along length or along width) and an order. The demand evidence is narrower than for basic grain, and it is concentrated on drawer banks, so start there.

### Feature 2: cut order as Auto / Favor / Always

Adopt Microvellum's scale because it is the only documented model that offers both soft and hard behavior, and phrase it in the vocabulary that matches the sheet: **Auto, Favor rip-first, Always rip-first, Favor crosscut-first, Always crosscut-first** when the sheet has grain, falling back to "lengthwise strips first / crosswise strips first" on grainless sheets, where "rip" has no meaning. Default to Auto, as every tool with a confirmed default does.

Map it onto the existing ranking without rewriting the solver. "Always" filters the candidate set to solutions whose first-stage cuts all run in the chosen direction. "Favor" keeps every candidate but adds first-cut conformance as a ranking criterion after sheet count, so it never costs a sheet. Whether it should also rank above waste is a product decision; ranking it after sheet count and before waste is a reasonable starting point. One prerequisite: today's `'horizontal-first'` and `'vertical-first'` rules split the same way at every node, so the solver needs either a true first-stage orientation (as PackingSolver models it) or a post-hoc classifier that reads each solution's top-level cut direction. The classifier is the cheaper first step and is enough to support filtering and ranking.

### Show what every constraint costs

This is the differentiator: no app found does it. After each solve, run the same job with the user's constraints relaxed (grain off for "Any orientation"-eligible parts, cut order set to Auto) and show the delta beside the result, for example "Grain on 14 parts: +1 sheet (+$68)" or "Always rip-first: no extra sheets". Use `pricePerSheet` when it is set and fall back to sheet count and waste percentage when it is not. Present it as information, not a warning, since pros routinely accept a few percent of material for faster cutting. A strong follow-up, if the comparison shows a cost, is to name which locked parts drive it ("unlocking the 4 back panels saves 1 sheet"), which turns CutPlan's "over-locking" advice into an actionable hint. The relaxed solve doubles solver work, so it should run after the primary result renders.

### Adjacent improvements the evidence supports

Two requests recur that neither feature covers. A **max rip width / fence capacity** field (constraining first-stage strip width when rip-first is active) has direct user demand and no hobby-tool implementation. **Strip grouping by width** with a cut sequence ordered by fence setting answers the "identical parts scattered" and "too many fence moves" complaints, and it reinforces rip-first rather than competing with it. The app's existing tie-break on orientation consistency of identical parts already points in this direction.

## Conclusion

The research reframes both features. Grain is not primarily a correctness feature that stops parts from being rotated wrongly; in user experience it is a **cost feature**, because the harm users report comes from constraints applied where they don't belong. Cut order is less about choosing rip versus crosscut than about **producing layouts that match a saw workflow**, which means first-stage direction, same-width strips and few fence settings working together. The apps that get grain structurally right (CutList Optimizer, OpenCutList, OptiCut) still leave the user blind to the price of their choices, and the one tool with a nuanced cut-order scale (Microvellum) is pro cabinet software that most hobbyists never see.

For cut-planner, the implication is that the decisive design element is the relaxed-solve comparison, not any single toggle. With a cost readout in place, a beginner can leave grain on everywhere and immediately see what it costs, and a pro on cheap melamine can choose "Always rip-first" knowing it costs nothing or one sheet. The current code has one latent bug to fix along the way: sheet grain along width is shown on screen but ignored by the solver.
